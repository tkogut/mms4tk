# Historical replay of past entries under a frozen B0 baseline

Source brief: `.ai/specs/briefs/2026-10-22-historical-replay-b0-baseline.md`

## 📝 TLDR

Two independent production samples (504 legs to 2026-10-14; 97 fresh legs, 2026-10-15–21) show the account's entire realized net loss is concentrated in STOP-LOSS exits (~6% of trades, 79–100%+ of the net loss), not in the protective rule layer. A structured debate (2026-10-21) concluded nobody currently knows whether the entry signal itself has directional edge, because the protective layer removes the "middle" of the outcome distribution before anyone can observe it. This spec designs a **read-only** replay: re-apply a frozen, simpler exit policy (B0) to the real historical entries and compare its simulated result against what actually happened, to answer that one question. It does not decide the broader "simplify the system" debate — that stays explicitly out of scope.

## 📝 Problem Statement

- Historical sample (504 legs to 2026-10-14, `.ai/analysis/2026-10-14-sl-loss-attribution.md`): STOP-LOSS = 30 legs (6%) = −79.83 USD net, total account result −34.88 USD net (everything else nets ~+45). Aggregate edge not statistically distinguishable from zero (t≈1.5, 95% CI [−0.087, +0.090] per-close gross).
- Fresh out-of-sample (97 legs, 2026-10-15–21): STOP-LOSS = 6 legs (6%) = −22.04 USD net of −27.80 total (79% of the loss from 6% of trades) — same pattern, independently reproduced.
- A two-round adversarial debate (2026-10-21) found the per-exit-reason P&L table cannot clear the protective layer, because the layer decides which trade lands in which bucket. Direct code reading (`src/mms4tk/inactivity_manager.py:371-568`) confirmed a real asymmetry: the Profit Giveback/Stall rule fires only on `profit_atr >= 0.6` (winners only); a losing position that moves past the stagnation-compression band has **no** inactivity-based exit at all and rides unprotected to the single hard 2% stop-loss, which arrives late (6.6–15h after entry). The 51-leg "pure TP/SL" subsample some reasoning defaulted to is itself selection-biased toward fast-moving entries and was explicitly retracted as a usable answer.
- The 2026-10-14 analysis named this fix (recommendation #5) a week before this spec and it has sat unexecuted; the fresh 97-leg sample reproducing the identical pattern makes it overdue.

## 📝 Proposed Solution

Build a one-off, read-only Python script (following the `.ai/analysis/` lineage — a dated diagnostic, not a maintained product feature) that, for each historical position:

1. Identifies the position's real entry (symbol, side, entry price, entry timestamp) from the actual historical record — **not** by regenerating signals from scratch (see Alternatives Considered).
2. Walks forward through that symbol's actual historical candles (from `ohlcv_cache`, falling back to a read-only OKX historical-candle fetch if cache coverage is insufficient) applying a single, pre-registered, frozen exit policy **B0**: fixed stop-loss (the existing `base_sl_pct`, unchanged — this replay tests removing the protective *exit* layer, not re-tuning the stop size), take-profit at the **opposite TMA band** — recomputed every forward candle via the production `TMAIndicator.calculate()` (unchanged), exactly matching the live system's own "Take Profit on opposite TMA band" mechanism (`daemon.py`'s `MMS4 Rule 1.4 & 8.4` log line) — a short's target is the lower band, a long's target is the upper band, **not** the static `tma_mid` midline (that level belongs only to the separate, newer `MIDLINE_PARTIAL_TP` feature and is not part of B0). No STALE_SPLIT_50/FORCE_CLOSE/BREAK-EVEN/addon/H4-gate machinery.
3. Records whichever of TP or SL is touched first (by candle high/low, matching how a real order would trigger intrabar) as B0's simulated exit, or marks the position **censored** if neither is touched before the historical data runs out.
4. Runs **two** pre-registered statistical tests, not one: (i) **does B0 itself show edge** — B0's simulated net USD per position against zero (this is the test that most directly answers "does the entry signal have measurable edge," independent of what the live system currently does), and (ii) **is B0 better or worse than reality** — B0's simulated net USD paired against the actual realized net USD for the same position (summed across its real legs, so a split position's multiple legs don't double-count its one outcome). These are related but distinct questions; the report keeps them in separate, clearly labeled sections rather than collapsing them into one headline number.
5. (Phase 2) Repeats the same walk-forward simulation with a second exit policy, **B0+adverse-brake**, which adds one additional exit rule: a direct mirror of the Profit Giveback/Stall rule onto the loss side (an adverse excursion reaching the mirrored ATR threshold that then stalls near that extreme for the mirrored candle count triggers an exit) — testing whether the asymmetry found in the live code is itself a fixable gap, entirely inside the replay.
6. Reports facts, limitations, and recommendations in the same style and statistical rigor as `.ai/analysis/2026-10-14-sl-loss-attribution.md` (paired comparison, sign test / confidence interval, explicit sample size, explicit caveats) — never a bare "B0 wins" headline without the uncertainty that earns it.

### Alternatives considered

- **Regenerate entries from scratch** by re-running `MMS4SignalEngine` against historical candles under B0, instead of taking real historical entries as given. Rejected: this would require faithfully reproducing every piece of live signal-generation state (SCOUT_MODE sizing, post-SL cooldowns, the H4 directional gate, candle-close gating) that could cause which signals fire to silently diverge from reality — risking an answer to "is our entry-generation code re-implementable" instead of "would the trades that actually happened have done better under a simpler exit policy," which is the question this spec exists to answer.
- **Build a general-purpose backtesting engine** (a `Strategy`/`Broker` class hierarchy, pluggable data feeds, order-book simulation, à la backtrader/vectorbt/Zipline). Rejected as over-engineering for a one-off diagnostic: this repo's `.ai/analysis/` convention is a dated script producing a dated report, not a maintained library, and there is exactly one strategy and two exit-policy variants to compare here, not an open-ended set.
- **Build nothing; keep relying on the per-exit-reason P&L table.** Rejected — this is literally the position the debate's own main-thread participant started from and retracted once checked against the code; the table cannot distinguish "the layer is neutral" from "the layer hides the answer," and two independent live samples now show the same dominant-loss pattern without resolving it.

## 📝 Architecture

**Component**: a single new script, e.g. `.ai/scripts/b0_baseline_replay.py` (or `scripts/`, matching whichever the implementer finds `.ai/analysis/2026-10-14-sl-loss-attribution.md`'s own companion script lives under — not yet located precisely; Phase 1 Step 1 confirms the convention). No daemon, API, or production-path changes.

**Reused, unchanged, production code** (never forked/reimplemented — reuse is the point, since B0 must share the real system's entry definition and band math exactly):
- `mms4tk.analytics.TMAIndicator.calculate()` — the opposite-band target.
- `mms4tk.analytics.Candle` / `IndicatorParams` — shared data shapes.
- `mms4tk.logger_db.LoggerDB` opened read-only (`mode=ro`, matching the 2026-10-14 analysis's own convention) — never a write-capable connection.
- The existing `base_sl_pct` system setting (read once, not re-derived) for B0's fixed stop size.

**New, script-local logic**:
- Position reconstruction (Data Model below). Note: `position_snapshots` (`src/mms4tk/logger_db.py:233-241`) is **not** used for this — it is `PRIMARY KEY (symbol, side)`, holding only the current live snapshot per symbol/side, overwritten on every update, with no historical record at all. Grouping instead reuses the existing, already-correct `cycle_logs`-based `entry_timestamp` derivation inside `LoggerDB.get_closed_trades()`, cross-checked against `okx_orders`.
- The B0 (and Phase 2 B0+adverse-brake) forward-walk simulator: given a position's entry and a candle series, returns the first-touch exit and simulated PnL.
- The paired comparison / report writer.

**Data flow**: `closed_trades` + `cycle_logs` (read) → position reconstruction → `ohlcv_cache` (read, or OKX historical fetch fallback) per position's forward window → B0 simulator → per-position paired result → aggregate statistics → markdown report + machine-readable sidecar.

## 📝 Data Model

No schema changes. Reads only:

- **`closed_trades`**: `symbol`, `side`, `entry_price`, `exit_price`, `pnl_net_usd`, `timestamp` (exit time), `entry_timestamp` (already derived and cached by `LoggerDB.get_closed_trades()` via a `cycle_logs` join on the nearest prior `outcome LIKE 'EXECUTED_BASE%'` row — reuse this existing, already-correct derivation rather than re-deriving it).
- **Position grouping**: group `closed_trades` rows sharing the same `(symbol, side, entry_timestamp)` into one position — this is exactly how a split position's multiple exit legs (e.g. one `STALE_SPLIT_50` leg + one later `STALE_FORCE_CLOSE`/`STOP-LOSS` leg) already share one entry in the existing data, per the 2026-10-14 analysis's own grouping note. `entry_timestamp` is the known-reliable key only from ~2026-09-18 onward (one value spans 24–30 legs before that, per the same analysis) — **Phase 1 Step 1 empirically confirms the usable window** rather than assuming it; if the reliable window is materially smaller than hoped, the report discloses the resulting sample size and confidence loss explicitly rather than silently proceeding.
- **`okx_orders` cross-check**: the brief's original grouping proposal named `okx_orders` alongside `position_snapshots`. `position_snapshots` is confirmed unusable for this (see Architecture note below), but `okx_orders` (historical, keyed by `ord_id`, has `fill_time`/`pnl`/`fee`/`raw_json`) is real exchange-fill history and is used here as a **cross-check**, not the primary grouping source: for each `cycle_logs`-derived position grouping, verify that the number and timing of `okx_orders` fills for that `(symbol, posSide)` window is consistent with the expected leg count (e.g., a position with one `STALE_SPLIT_50` + one `STOP-LOSS` leg should show two corresponding reduceOnly fills). A mismatch is a data-quality signal — disclosed per-position in the sidecar, not silently resolved either way.
- **`ohlcv_cache`**: `(symbol, timeframe, timestamp) → OHLCV` for the forward-walk candles from each position's entry. Coverage must be verified in Phase 1 Step 1 (not assumed); gaps trigger the OKX historical-candle fallback (read-only market-data fetch, no account/order calls).
- **Addon-affected positions**: a position that had a real addon (pyramiding, per `position_addons`/the addon ledger) had a real notional larger than its own B0-simulated size (B0 explicitly excludes addon machinery, per the brief). Comparing B0's base-only simulated USD against the actual position's full blended-addon USD would silently compare two different notional sizes. **Decision**: report both an absolute-USD comparison (base-size-only on both sides — i.e. the actual-result comparator also uses only the position's base-leg PnL, excluding any addon leg's contribution, computed separately and disclosed) and a percentage-return comparison (scale-independent), and flag every addon-affected position explicitly in the per-position sidecar so a reader can audit the exclusion rather than trust a blended number.

**Outputs** (new files, not new tables):
- `.ai/analysis/{date}-b0-baseline-replay.md` — the report, in the existing analysis-doc style.
- A machine-readable sidecar (CSV or JSON) with one row per position: entry/exit timestamps, actual net USD, B0-simulated net USD, B0+adverse-brake-simulated net USD (Phase 2), censored flag, addon-affected flag — so every number in the report's aggregate statistics is independently auditable, not just asserted.

## 📝 API Contracts

None — this is an offline script, not a service. It calls no new endpoints and exposes none.

## 📝 UI/UX

None — the deliverable is a markdown report + sidecar, read by a human (the same consumption pattern as every existing `.ai/analysis/` document).

## 📝 Edge Cases & Failure Scenarios

- **Insufficient `ohlcv_cache` coverage for a position's forward window**: fall back to a read-only OKX historical-candle fetch; if that also fails or is rate-limited, skip the position and count it in a disclosed "excluded — no data" bucket rather than silently shrinking the denominator.
- **Neither TP nor SL touched before available data ends** ("censored"): exclude from the netted win/loss comparison, report the count and the reason separately — including a censored position as a silent zero would bias the metric toward whichever side censoring happens to favor.
- **Overlapping re-entries**: the real system may have closed and reopened a position on the same symbol/side faster than B0's simulated exit would have fired (since B0 removes the mechanisms that cause faster real exits). The B0 simulation for a given historical entry must walk its own candle path in isolation — it must **not** be truncated early just because the real system happened to open a new real position on the same symbol before B0's simulated exit would have triggered. Document this explicitly in the report's limitations section, since it means a later real position's own candle window may overlap with an earlier position's still-open B0 simulation — by design, not a bug.
- **Look-ahead bias**: `TMAIndicator.calculate()`'s formula (SMA-of-SMA, trailing) is causal by construction, but Phase 1 Step 1 includes an explicit check (not an assumption) that no centered/non-causal window is being read at simulation time, and that the B0 rule itself was fully written and committed (pre-registered) before Phase 1 Step 4 is run against the full dataset — "no peeking," per the brief's explicit requirement.
- **Fee/cost asymmetry**: both the Oct-14 and Oct-21 samples show transaction costs are material to the comparison. B0's simulated PnL must apply the same realistic fee assumption the real system pays (the observed historical taker-fee rate, or an explicit, disclosed approximation) — an edge found only by ignoring fees would not survive contact with the account the way `.ai/analysis/2026-10-14-sl-loss-attribution.md` already learned the hard way for the live system.

## 📝 Risks & Impact Review

- **Blast radius**: none on production — read-only against historical data and cached candles; no production source file is touched by this spec (if the implementation discovers it needs more than a read-only OKX market-data fetch, that is an explicit scope increase to flag, not something to assume mid-build, per the brief's Non-goals).
- **Statistical risk**: the sample, even at its largest plausible window, may still be underpowered to resolve an effect the size the 2026-10-14 analysis already found underpowered (800–1600 closes needed) — the report must say so plainly rather than overclaim a verdict from a smaller N.
- **Rollback**: not applicable — nothing is deployed; the script and its report are added, reviewable, and removable like any other file.
- **Process impact (not this spec's decision)**: the 2026-10-21 debate recommended freezing new/changed entry/exit/filter rules while a forward confirmation sample accumulates (not bug fixes, safety fixes, or risk-sizing changes). This spec's Phase 1 report surfaces that recommendation explicitly for the repository owner to accept or reject — it is not implemented or enforced by this spec.

## 📋 Phasing

- **Phase 1 — B0 core replay**: position reconstruction, data-coverage verification, the pre-registered B0 rule and decision thresholds, the forward-walk simulator, both pre-registered tests, the report, **and its own freeze/forward-sample recommendation**. This alone fully answers "does the entry signal show edge, and does removing the protective exit layer change the result" — independently shippable and valuable on its own, with a complete recommendation, not a placeholder deferred to Phase 2.
- **Phase 2 — B0+adverse-brake variant**: adds the mirrored loss-side exit rule as a second simulated policy inside the same report, directly testing the specific asymmetry the 2026-10-21 debate found in the live code, plus whatever additional recommendation that specific finding implies. Depends on Phase 1's simulator being generic enough to accept a second exit-rule set (a design constraint carried into Phase 1's steps, not bolted on after).

## 📋 Implementation Plan

### Phase 1: B0 core replay

1. **Verify data coverage.** Read-only queries against `ohlcv_cache` (date range per symbol/timeframe) and `cycle_logs`/`closed_trades` (how far back `entry_timestamp` grouping is reliable, extending the 2026-10-14 analysis's own finding rather than re-deriving it from scratch). Output: a short findings note fixing the actual sample window (resolves the brief's conditional post-09-18 question empirically) and documenting the OKX historical-fetch fallback's rate limits if it will be needed. *Testable as*: the note itself, with exact row/date-range counts cited.
2. **Position reconstruction.** Extend/reuse `LoggerDB.get_closed_trades()`'s existing `cycle_logs` join; group resulting legs by `(symbol, side, entry_timestamp)` into positions; separately flag addon-affected positions via `position_addons`/ledger data. *Testable as*: unit tests against known real cases (e.g., the documented 2026-10-15 XAG addon incident's position) asserting the correct leg grouping and addon flag.
3. **Pre-register the B0 rule and the decision thresholds.** Write and commit, as a section of this spec or a linked doc, **before** Step 4 runs against the full dataset (satisfies the brief's "no peeking" requirement structurally, not just by stated intent):
   - The exact rule definition: TP = opposite TMA band (recomputed per candle, per the Proposed Solution section above); SL = `base_sl_pct` anchored to entry price; no stale/BE/addon/gate; the fee assumption used.
   - The exact decision thresholds the brief requires (brief: "which outcome triggers which decision"): e.g. what p-value/CI and effect-size combination counts as "B0 shows edge," what counts as "B0 beats actual," what counts as "inconclusive — need more data" (extend the 2026-10-14 analysis's own stop/go criteria — ~800–1600 closes for an effect this size — rather than inventing new thresholds).
4. **Forward-walk B0 simulator.** Given a position's entry and its candle series, return the first-touch exit (TP/SL/censored) using candle high/low for touch detection and the triggered level as the assumed fill price (a standard, conservative backtesting convention — not the candle's close). *Testable as*: unit tests with synthetic candle series covering TP-first, SL-first, both-in-the-same-candle (documented tie-break rule needed — e.g., assume the worse outcome for B0 when both are touched in one candle, since intrabar sequence is unknown — state this explicitly), and never-touched (censored).
5. **Compute and report both pre-registered metrics.** (i) B0's simulated net USD per position vs. zero (does the entry signal show measurable edge at all), and (ii) B0 vs. actual, paired per position (does the simpler policy beat the current one) — each with the same statistical treatment as the 2026-10-14 analysis (explicit n, sign test / CI, limitations), classified against the Step 3 decision thresholds. *Testable as*: both computation functions unit-tested against small hand-built fixtures with known expected statistics.
6. **Write the report**, including the Step-3-committed recommendation on whether the data supports a strategy-layer freeze while a forward confirmation sample accumulates (a recommendation, never a mandate — the repository owner decides) and what that forward sample's minimum size should be, extending the 2026-10-14 analysis's own stop/go criteria. `.ai/analysis/{date}-b0-baseline-replay.md` plus the per-position sidecar. *Testable as*: the report is generated from the Step 5 output with no manual editing of the numbers, and the sidecar lets a reviewer recompute every headline statistic independently. **This is where Phase 1 becomes independently shippable and valuable on its own**, per the Phasing section — a Phase-1-only report still carries its own recommendation, not a placeholder deferred to Phase 2.

### Phase 2: B0+adverse-brake variant

7. **Implement the mirrored adverse-move exit rule**, reusing Phase 1's simulator with a second, pluggable exit-rule set (the design constraint Phase 1 must satisfy). The ATR/stall-counting math mirrors `inactivity_manager.py`'s Profit Giveback/Stall rule (same thresholds, loss side instead of profit side) — **mirror the formula as new, script-local logic; do not import or instantiate the live `InactivityManager`/`PositionTracker` classes**, which are stateful and wired to live position tracking, not a historical forward-walk. State the mirrored formula explicitly before coding it, same pre-registration discipline as Step 3. *Testable as*: unit tests mirroring Step 4's fixtures, now exercising the new early-exit condition.
8. **Extend the report** to a three-way comparison (actual / B0 / B0+adverse-brake), re-running both pre-registered tests from Step 5 (vs. zero, and paired vs. actual) for the new variant too. *Testable as*: the sidecar gains the third column; the report's aggregate statistics section visibly covers all three, for both tests.
9. **Variant-specific recommendation.** Whether the adverse-brake variant's results justify pursuing the asymmetry fix as a real (not simulated) change — the general freeze/forward-sample recommendation already shipped in Phase 1 (Step 6); this step only adds what Phase 2's own new finding specifically implies, so it isn't duplicated.
